Two small pieces of money-handling code. Amounts and balances are whole numbers from 0 up to a fixed maximum, 18,446,744,073,709,551,615 (the largest unsigned 64-bit number, called 'the maximum'). Nothing can be negative or above the maximum.

The wallet. A wallet holds one balance. Deposit adds an amount; if the new balance would be above the maximum, the deposit is rejected with an 'overflow' error. Withdraw takes an amount off; if the amount is more than the balance, it is rejected with an 'insufficient funds' error. A wallet transfer moves an amount from a sender wallet to a receiver wallet, which is always a different wallet; it is rejected with 'insufficient funds' if the sender has less than the amount, or with 'overflow' if the receiver's balance would go above the maximum. Each call either succeeds or returns one error. There is no list of all wallets: a rule is about the wallets it mentions.

The ledger. A ledger is a list of balances, one per account. Accounts are numbered from 0, so a ledger with three accounts has accounts 0, 1 and 2; balances are written account 0 first, as in [100, 20, 0]. A transfer names a sender account, a receiver account and an amount; 0->1:30 moves 30 from account 0 to account 1. A transfer is valid on a ledger when both accounts exist, they are different accounts, the sender's balance is at least the amount, and the receiver's balance plus the amount is at most the maximum. When a transfer is applied, the ledger either accepts it and moves the money, or rejects it. A batch is a list of transfers applied in order: it stops at the first transfer that is rejected, keeps the ones already applied, and reports how many it applied. Applying a batch as a whole is all or nothing and reports accepted or rejected. Computing the total adds up all the balances and gives nothing if the sum is above the maximum. Looking up an account gives its balance, or nothing if there is no such account.

Words. Covers: the balance is at least the amount. Room: the new balance would be at most the maximum. Internal transfer: its sender and its receiver are both accounts of this ledger. Aggregate: added up over all of the ledger's accounts. The total, or combined balance, of some balances: their sum. Added as plain numbers: ordinary addition with no upper limit and no wrap-around.

How rules are read. 'Never' and 'always' cover every call, including calls the operation rejects, unless the rule itself narrows them (for example 'a successful withdrawal'). A change is judged just before and just after the operation. Every operation must also return an answer: no crash, no hang. An echo restates a rule in four slots: Applies to (which calls are covered), Assuming (conditions), Then (what must hold), and Defaults applied (what the tool filled in that the rule left unsaid).
