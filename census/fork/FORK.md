# Aeneas fork spike: four small patches (and a fifth), measured on the census corpus

Generated by `scripts/build_fork_md.py` from `parts/`, `patches/`, `repro/` and `data/`. Do not edit `FORK.md`; edit those and rerun. Run date 2026-11-10, finalised the same evening by a second pass that reran nothing heavy, macOS arm64, on a machine shared with other jobs (load average 90 to 200 for most of the run; timings are not reported as results).

## Result in brief

- **Build from source works.** Aeneas `0d21a9ec` (the nightly-2026.11.10 release) with Charon `454586e9` built in about ten minutes of setup, most of it compiling OCaml 5.3.0 and its packages; the Aeneas build itself takes 40 seconds. The unpatched source build reproduces the census as shipped: 64 of 4385 functions, 307 lines, and the same status for every function of `shlex`, `untrusted`, `fnv` and `zeroize`.
- **As shipped, the four patches take the corpus from 64 functions (1%) to 1549 (35%) that end as Lean that compiles; lines from 307 (1%) to 10337 (23%).** Nine crates without cedar: 64 of 1882 (3%) to 1549 (82%).
- **Where the gain comes from.** Patch (ii), the trait-method signature guard, gives +1368 functions: the five crates that produced nothing now produce exactly the functions the census reached by manual peeling (same set, function for function). Patch (iv), name capture, gives +117. Patches (i) and (iii) give +0 on these ten crates as shipped: they matter for cedar, which as shipped still stops earlier, in Charon.
- **Cedar, as shipped: still 0.** Charon dies on a rustc internal compiler error before Aeneas runs; no Aeneas patch can change that.
- **Cedar with only the Charon-side workarounds** (49 functions opaque, two generated parsers opaque; nothing removed on the Aeneas side; reported apart from the as-shipped tables): unpatched and with (i) or (ii) Aeneas aborts with no output. With (i)-(iv) it writes 400144 lines of Lean and **996 of 2503 functions (40%) end as Lean that compiles, 6269 lines**; the census reached 37 with 21 manual removals.
- **Not measured.** Cedar with the extra patch (v), and the joined run of cedar with (i)-(iii): both runs were started, did not finish in over six hours of wall clock on a loaded machine that was asleep for most of them, and were killed. They were not rerun. See "Not measured" in the cedar section for the partial evidence and what a bounded rerun needs.
- **Aeneas's own test suite: no regression.** 122 extraction tests, all exit 0 before and after every patch; generated outputs are byte-identical except two expected-error files that quote an Aeneas source line number moved by patch (iii). The suite runs with `-abort-on-error`, under which the new handlers are inactive, so it shows the patches change nothing they should not; it does not exercise the new paths. The reproductions and the census do.
- **Soundness sample: 25 functions run in Rust and in the generated Lean on 895 generated inputs, 0 mismatches** (68 of the inputs panic in Rust and fail in Lean alike). Only 6 of the 30 functions in the seeded sample the task asked for could be run; the rest of the 25 come from two further seeded strata. It is a thin check, mostly small arithmetic and accessors.
- **New along the way.** A 30-line single-file reproduction of the uncaught-exception crash that took the census 129 runs to bisect on cedar (`repro/crash_stack_of_refs.rs`); and a small over-count in the census's two stricter columns (3 functions, see "Soundness sample").
- **Recommendation, as a trade:** carry the five commits as a fork now and send the five issue texts in `upstream/` (written, not sent); drop each commit as upstream fixes it. Details at the end.

## What was built, and how to rerun

- **Toolchain.** `brew install opam pkgconf`; an opam root inside `census/fork/.opam` (not `~/.opam`) with OCaml 5.3.0 and the packages Aeneas and charon-ml list. `source census/fork/env.sh` sets it up. Charon was built from source at the pin for Aeneas's tests; on `shlex` its LLBC is identical to the release binary's apart from the output path stored in the file. The census runs use the release Charon binary, as the census did.
- **Commit.** `0d21a9ec`, as asked. Its only source difference from the census's main binary (`5a392f4b`) is three keywords in `LeanKeywords.ml`. The generated Lean is compiled against the Lean library of the 11.09 release on Lean v4.35.0, the census's setup, because the 11.10 library needs a newer Lean and Mathlib than are pinned here.
- **Pipeline.** `scripts/fork_census.py` imports `census/scripts/census.py` unchanged, redirects its paths under `census/fork/runs/<variant>-<mode>/` and switches the peel off: one Charon run, one Aeneas run, nothing marked or removed. The join and the definition of "ends as compiling Lean" are the census's. Denominators are the census's counted functions and lines.
- **Variants.** `base` (unpatched), `p1` … `p4` (the four patches asked for, applied cumulatively in the order asked), `p5` (plus the extra patch (v)). Binaries in `bin/` (git-ignored).

### What still blocks cedar after the patches

In the order a run meets them.

1. **Charon: the rustc internal compiler error (49 sites).** Unchanged; as shipped nothing of cedar reaches Aeneas. The census's analysis stands (`IMPROVEMENTS.md`, `ice`): the failing code is days old, and hax 0.5.0, which pins a Charon from 2026-10-31, gets through. The options are an older Charon pin or a Charon fix; neither was tried here.
2. **The two generated LALRPOP parsers** still have to be opaque for Charon, or Aeneas does not finish in reasonable time and memory. Unchanged.
3. **Mixed declaration groups themselves, and everything that calls into them.** Cedar has 85 such groups in this LLBC. Patch (i) stops them from failing the rest of the crate, but the functions in a group get no analysis result, and Aeneas's analysis then fails every function that calls one of them ("The function called here is missing from the crate", 361 function groups), and so on up the call graph. In the run with patches (i)-(iii), 890 function signatures fail with the resulting "Internal error" (`SymbolicToPureTypes.ml`, line 724), and the groups cannot be printed either ("Mixed-recursive declaration groups are not supported", 85). This is now the largest single cause, and it is a feature gap (types and functions that are mutually recursive through trait impls), not a missing recursive call. A cheaper intermediate step exists and was not tried: give the functions of a mixed group the conservative analysis result (may fail, may diverge) instead of none, which would stop the cascade at the group.
4. **Function items and function pointers as values.** "Unimplemented" in `RegionsHierarchy.ml` line 302 (212 errors) and line 315 (32), `SymbolicToPureTypes.ml` line 398 (133), "Arrow types are not supported yet" (171): `fnitem` in `IMPROVEMENTS.md`. 169 trait instances fail to translate, most of them the `FnOnce`/`FnMut`/`Fn` impls rustc builds for a function passed by name.
5. **Trait objects.** "Dynamic trait types are not supported yet" (270 errors over three sites), "Unimplemented use of dynamic traits" (39). With patch (ii) the local traits with `dyn` in a method signature (`TCNode`, `Schema`, `EntityTypeDescription`, `miette::Diagnostic` …) lose that method instead of aborting the crate: 12 trait methods are dropped with a warning.
6. **Borrow patterns outside the fragment.** "Can't end abstraction … as it is set as non-endable" (209), "Inconsistent projection" (91), "Unreachable" in `InterpBorrowsCore.ml` (69).
7. **Higher-ranked lifetimes related to a free lifetime** (`TypesAnalysis.ml` line 990, 148 errors).
8. **Types that fail to translate, and their users.** 25 type declarations fail (for example `ast::extension::ExtensionFunction`, which holds a boxed closure); more than 240 "Could not find: type name …" errors follow at extraction.
9. **Exceptions that used to end the run, now caught.** 18 uncaught exceptions are reported against an item (6 `ty_regions` failures in the symbolic interpreter, 12 `Not_found`); 23 functions fail in the micro-passes and 8 in `add_type_annotations`. Each is one failed item instead of the whole crate.

## The patches

Branch `census-fork` in the local clone `census/fork/aeneas` (git-ignored): the four commits asked for, in the order asked, and a fifth that the cedar run showed was needed, on top of `0d21a9ec`. ### (i) `FunsAnalysis`: go on after a mixed declaration group — 6 lines

- **Defect.** `analyze_decl_groups` walks the declaration groups in order and recurses in every case except `MixedGroup`, where it logs "Mixed declaration groups … are not supported yet" and returns. Every function after the first such group has no analysis result and fails with "Internal error" when its signature is computed.
- **Change.** Recurse on the remaining groups after logging the error.

On cedar that cascade is the largest remaining blocker (see "What still blocks cedar").

### (ii) `Translate`: a trait method signature that fails marks that method, not the crate — 78 lines

- **Defect.** All signatures are computed before any body. The loop over function declarations catches Aeneas's error type (`CFailure`); the one over trait methods (`translate_method_sig`) does not, so one method signature ends the run with no output. Seen triggers: `Iterator::copied`/`cloned`/`try_fold` (trait-type constraint), `Pattern::into_searcher` (generic associated type), local traits with `dyn` in a method.
- **Change, three parts.**
  1. Catch the error per trait method, log a warning that names the trait and the method, and remove the method from the set of methods to extract, so that the trait declaration is translated without it.
  2. When translating a trait impl, ignore the methods that are no longer extracted for its trait. Without this the impl would define a field the declaration lacks.

### (iii) Catch exceptions that are not `CFailure`, per function and per declaration group — 129 lines

- **Defect.** Aeneas signals errors with `CFailure`, and its per-item handlers match only that. Helper code also raises `Failure`, `Not_found` and `Invalid_argument`; one of those in one function ends the run with no output and no function name.
- **Change.** A predicate `Errors.exn_is_recoverable` (every exception except `Out_of_memory` and `Sys.Break`; none at all under `-abort-on-error`) and `Errors.save_uncaught_exn`, which registers an error "Uncaught exception: …" against the item. Handlers added in five places:
  1. `Interp.evaluate_function_symbolic` (symbolic execution of one body): the function is kept with an error body, as for `CFailure`.
  2. `Translate.translate_function_to_pure`: the function is dropped, as for `CFailure`.
  3. `PureMicroPasses.apply_passes_to_pure_fun_translations`: the micro-passes had no per-function handler at all. On failure the body is replaced by an error node and the passes are applied to that (the function is then extracted as `def … := sorry`); if that fails too the function is made opaque (an `axiom`).
  4. `PureMicroPassesAnnots.add_type_annotations`: same arm as its existing `CFailure` handler (the declaration is kept without annotations and an error is registered).
  5. `Translate.extract_definitions`: an uncaught exception in one declaration group no longer loses the file.
- **Wider than the task's wording.** The task names the micro-passes. On cedar the first uncaught exception is in the symbolic interpreter (1), the next in the micro-passes (3), then in `add_type_annotations` (4), then in the extraction (5); each was found by running cedar and reading where it died. All five are in the one commit because any one of them alone leaves cedar with no output.
- **What a caught function looks like.** `sorry` or `axiom`, plus an error in the log. The census join counts both as "failed at Aeneas", so this patch cannot add a function to the "compiles" column by hiding a failure; it adds the functions *around* the failed one.
- **Left to patch (v).** After an exception in the middle of printing a definition the open formatting boxes are not closed. That is the existing `CFailure` behaviour, and this patch makes it reachable more often.
- **Expected-output churn.** Two `.borrow-check.out` files quote the Aeneas source line of a warning that moved (`Interp.ml, line 686` to `680`); the commit updates them.

### (iv) Lean backend: two kinds of name capture — 109 lines

- **Defect 1.** Builtin types and the functions in their namespace are printed by short name (`Slice`, `Slice.len`, `Array.index_usize`, `Result` …) and found through `open Aeneas.Std`. A user declaration `X.Slice` takes precedence in every definition whose name starts with `X.`, beginning with `structure X.Slice … where entries : Slice …` itself.

A first version qualified on any declaration, external ones included; `core::result::Result` is in nearly every crate, so that rewrote `Result` in 39 test outputs and was narrowed.

### (v) Extraction: reset the formatter after an error — 14 lines (extra, not in the task)

- **Defect.** Aeneas prints through OCaml's `Format` boxes. When an exception interrupts the printing of a definition, the boxes open at that point are never closed. Every later definition is printed indented inside the unfinished one; after enough of them the indentation reaches the margin and each token goes on its own line, at the column of the `def` keyword. Lean requires the tokens of a signature to be to the right of `def`, so from that point on every definition is a parse error ("unexpected token '('; expected ':=', 'where' or '|'"). The census had noted the indentation as cosmetic on `time`; on cedar, with patches (i)-(iv), 178 counted functions end in a Lean parse error and most declaration lines of the file are indented.
- **How it was found.** Parse errors are one of the three large Lean-stage classes of the cedar run with patches (i)-(iv). It existed before (the handler for `CFailure` has the same gap), but only a crate with many extraction errors gets far enough to the right.
- **Change.** In the per-declaration-group handler of `extract_definitions`, for both the existing `CFailure` arm and the new one: `Format.pp_print_newline` (which closes every open box) and re-open the top-level vertical box that `extract_file` expects.
- **Effect.** None on Aeneas's test outputs and none on the counts of the nine other crates as shipped. On `time` the generated file is no longer indented (see "Not measured" above for the figures). **On cedar its effect was not measured:** the run with (i)-(v) did not finish.

## Soundness sample

**Question.** The patches make Aeneas emit Lean for functions it used to drop with the rest of the crate. Is that Lean a faithful model of the Rust? Counting says nothing about that.

**Method** (`scripts/sound_run.py`, results in `data/sound_results.json`, raw outputs in `data/sound/`).

- **Rust side.** A copy of the crate gets, at the end of the source file of each sampled function, a module holding one exported function per case, so private functions and fields are reachable without changing them. A small binary links the copy and calls them. Dev profile: integer overflow panics, which is the semantics Aeneas models. A panic is caught and printed as `PANIC`.
- **Lean side.** `lake` writes no `.olean` for a module with an error, so a generated file with one failing declaration cannot be imported at all. The Lean file per crate therefore holds the header of the generated file and then, verbatim and in the original order, the declarations in the dependency closure of the sampled functions (found by scanning identifiers), followed by one `#eval` per input. The result is observed with the library's own `Result.match`; `fail` is printed as `PANIC`. One batch `lake build` per crate; no per-input process.
- **Inputs.** Up to 40 per function from `random.Random(20261110…)`: a mix of edge values (0, 1, maximum, sign bit) and uniform values, byte strings of length 0 to 6, all seven variants for an enum. For `increase_scale64` the multiplier is drawn both from powers of ten (its documented use) and from arbitrary values, so that the overflow path is exercised.
- **Comparison.** Text equality of a canonical rendering written by hand for each function (numbers in decimal, arrays as lists, `Decimal` as its 16 serialised bytes).

**Result.**

- **25 functions run on both sides, 895 inputs, 0 mismatches.** 68 of the inputs panic in Rust and fail in Lean alike (overflow in `increase_scale64` and `unscale_from_overflow`, index out of bounds in the two `Index` impls).
- **Sample A** (the one asked for): 6 of 30 run. Of the other 24, 14 call a function of the crate that did not translate, 6 depend on an unmodelled external item, and 4 are closed but take or return a type with no constructible value on the Lean side (`core::fmt::Formatter` is `opaque`; `time`'s date and offset types hold `deranged` ranged integers, which are axioms in the generated Lean) or were not harnessed.
- **Samples B and C** add 10 and 9 functions. One function of sample B, `str::handle_separator`, turned out not to be runnable although the census join marks it closed (next paragraph).
- **What was covered.** Bit and limb arithmetic of `rust_decimal` (shifts, casts, widening multiplies, a subtract-with-borrow loop over slices, a rounding rescale with a nested call), two enum functions and a string-holding constructor of `time`, and for patch (iv) the functions it unlocks in `untrusted` (the renamed parameter in `Reader::new`, the qualified `Aeneas.Std.Slice`) and `indexmap` (length, emptiness and indexing of the `Slice` types that used to capture the builtin name).
- **What was not.** Anything with a formatter, an iterator adaptor, a hash map, a closure argument or one of `time`'s main types; no function of `semver` or `rusty-money`; nothing from cedar. The functions that could be run are the simple end of the population. Zero mismatches in 25 small functions is evidence that the patches do not corrupt the output, and weak evidence about Aeneas's translation in general.

**Which build.** The samples were drawn on the build with patches (i)-(iv), and the figures above are for its Lean text. Patch (v) was added afterwards and changes line breaks and indentation after an extraction error; the whole check was rerun on the Lean text of the (i)-(v) build with the same result, 25 functions, 895 inputs, 0 mismatches (`SOUND_VARIANT=p5`, `data/sound_results_p5.json`).

**Why the patches cannot easily make a wrong function count as compiling.** (i) and (ii) change which items are translated, not how. (iii) turns a crash into `sorry` or an `axiom` plus a logged error, and the census join counts all three as failed. (iv) changes names only: a local variable gets a fresh name, a builtin gets its full name; a wrong resolution would have to type-check by accident. Sample C is the direct check of (iv). (v) changes line breaks and indentation only.

**A defect in the census join, found here.** `str::handle_separator` is marked "every local callee compiles, no unmodelled external" in the census data, but its mutual-recursion group calls `str::handle_full_128`, whose body is `sorry`, and `str::maybe_round`, which Aeneas emitted as an axiom. The closure flags in `census/scripts/census.py` (`clean`) are computed with a provisional "true" for functions on the current recursion stack, and that provisional value is cached for the other members of a cycle. Recomputed without the shortcut on the census's own data, "every local callee compiles" is 933 functions, not 936, and "no unmodelled external" is 379, not 382; all three are in `rust_decimal`'s parser. The headline "Lean compiles" column is not affected. Not fixed: outside this task's write scope.

## Recommendation: fork or upstream, as a trade

**What each route buys and costs.**

| | carry a fork | upstream only |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Gain on this corpus | immediate: 64 to 1549 functions as shipped (1% to 35%); nine crates 3% to 82% | the same, on the day the fixes are merged and released; not before, and the date is not ours |
| Cost to obtain | done: five commits, about 300 lines, `patches/0001…0005` | five issue texts (written, in `upstream/`, not sent), and probably pull requests: the maintainers may want (ii) and (iv) done differently |
| Cost to keep | a rebase each time the pinned Aeneas moves; a source build (opam, OCaml 5.3, 40 s) on every machine and in CI instead of a release tarball; we own the patches' correctness | none |
| Coupling | inherits the release's coupling: one Charon commit, one Rust nightly, one Lean and Mathlib pin per Aeneas commit | same coupling, handled upstream |
| Risk | drift if the fork accumulates; the soundness check behind it is 25 small functions | upstream declines or redesigns a patch and the gain for that part never arrives in this form |

**Facts that bear on it.**

- **The patches are small and local.** (i) is one recursive call. (ii) and (iii) add handlers next to handlers that already exist, in the style of the surrounding code. (iv) is the only one with a design choice in it (when to qualify a builtin name).
- **Rebase cost so far.** Between the two releases the census used (one day apart) the only source change was three lines in a keyword list; the five commits apply to both (checked with `git am`). Aeneas does move fast (a release every night), but the fork only has to follow the releases this project pins, not every night's.
- **Upstream's test outputs do not move**, apart from two line numbers. That makes the patches cheap to review and cheap to carry.
- **The fork does not fix cedar.** As shipped cedar is still 0, because of Charon. With the Charon workarounds the patches turn "no output" into a large Lean file (one measurement, with (i)-(iv); the effect of (v) on cedar was not measured), and what is left is feature work (mixed groups, function items, trait objects), not robustness. A fork that goes after those is a different, much larger commitment than these five commits.
- **Nothing here is private.** All five are defects any user of Aeneas on a real crate will hit; upstream has an interest in them. The issue texts in `upstream/` come with reproductions of 10 to 70 lines, except the formatter one, which has none.

**Recommendation.** Do both, in this order, and keep the fork thin.

1. **Use the fork now** for the census, the profile and plan node S1: it is what makes five of the ten crates usable at all, with no manual peeling. Pin it as "release X plus `patches/`", applied by script, so that the fork is five files and not a diverging repository.
2. **Send issues 1, 2, 3 and 5 first** (mixed-group recursion, method-signature abort, uncaught exceptions, formatter reset). They are bug reports with one-file reproductions and small fixes; if upstream merges them, four of the five commits disappear at the next pin.
3. **Send issue 4 as a question plus a patch** (name capture): upstream may prefer always-qualified builtins or a different renaming rule. Carry our version until they decide.
4. **Do not grow the fork into feature work** (mixed groups proper, function items, `dyn`) without a separate decision. Those are weeks to months, they touch Aeneas's core, and they are where being upstream matters most.

**The trade in one line:** the fork costs a source build and a rebase per pin and buys 1485 functions today; upstreaming costs five reports, already written, and an unknown wait and removes the carrying cost for good. The two are not alternatives unless upstream declines.

**Sending is the owner's step.** Nothing was posted, no issue or pull request was opened, and nothing was pushed (the clone's push URL is disabled).
