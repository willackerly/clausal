# H2 — do closed sub-statements recur across proofs?

Generated by `bench/reuse.py` from the Lean dump of `Rosette/Reuse.lean`. Every number below is computed from `out/reuse/*.jsonl`; sizes are DAG sizes of kernel terms.

## Verdict on H2

**Raw recurrence is common; reuse worth mining was not found in human-written Mathlib.**

1. *Recurrence exists.* 4122 closed statements occur in ≥ 2 of the 600 theorems; they cover 20.3% of all candidate occurrences and touch 508 theorems. Replacing every repeat by a call would remove at most 145759 nodes, 12.5% of the proof terms (upper estimate).
2. *Almost none of it is a discovery.* 98.6% of the recurring statements fall in a trivial class. 2314 (56.1%) are machine output inside the human proof (typeclass instances, `simp`/`rw` congruence plumbing, `ring`/`linarith`/`norm_num` certificates) and carry 68.3% of the per-class saving. 1718 (41.7%) are a library lemma or trivially closed: same hash as an existing theorem, closed by `rfl` or `exact?`, a proof that uses at most one library lemma, or closed by one call of a standard tactic.
3. *What is left is small, local, and on inspection not new mathematics.* 59 statements (1.4%) survive every filter, including the one-tactic probe; 18 of them would save anything, together 2354 nodes = **0.2%** of the proof terms. For 16 of those `exact?` finished without finding a library lemma, for 2 it ran out of budget. 14 of the 18 live inside one file, 1 cross slices, and 9 have the identical proof term at every occurrence. The largest three by saving are: `Complex.re ⁻¹' Set.Ioi 1 ∈ nhds 2` (538 nodes, 4 theorems); `∀ {V : Type u_1} (G : SimpleGraph V) {s t : V} [inst : DecidableEq V] [inst_1 : Fintype V] [inst_2 : DecidableRel G.Adj], G.edgeFinset.card  …` (299 nodes, 2 theorems); `∀ {α : Type u_1} {𝕜 : Type u_2} [inst : Field 𝕜] [inst_1 : LinearOrder 𝕜] [inst_2 : IsStrictOrderedRing 𝕜] (G : SimpleGraph α) [inst_3 : Dec …` (251 nodes, 4 theorems). These are side computations repeated between sibling theorems of one file, or rewriting residue that the filters do not recognise; the full list is the second top-25 table. An independent review of the first version of this analysis drew a seeded sample of 10 of its net-positive 'substantive' statements and found that 9 closed with one tactic or one library lemma and the tenth was a two-lemma rewrite; that is why the one-tactic probe was added. None of the 18 that remain was shown to be a lemma worth naming, and none was examined by a mathematician; several are one-line consequences of an existing lemma that the probes do not find. The realistic number of unnamed lemmas found by exact matching is small and may be zero.
4. *Generalisation does not rescue it.* Abstracting literals and instances (L1) adds 1060 clusters, but they are numeral facts (`0 < 4`, `4 * 0 = 0`); after removing instance / one-lemma / automation / ground-fact / whole-theorem-body clusters, 13 tight ones remain, worth 1094 nodes if their generalisations are provable. The depth-2 shape (L2) gives 74 non-trivial clusters with an optimistic 23704 nodes, but most anti-unifiers are vacuous (`◻ * ◻ = ◻ * ◻`); only 24 are tight (3083 nodes = 0.3%). 9 L2 clusters with positive saving (17158 nodes) and 1 at L1 are excluded as whole-theorem-body clusters: their members are nearly the entire proofs of sibling theorems that Mathlib states and names separately. The first version of this report presented one of them (`∃ p, 0 < p ∧ ◻ =O[atTop] fun x => exp (-p * x)`, four Hurwitz kernels) as a real parametrised lemma. It is not: the four are the named theorems `isBigO_atTop_evenKernel_sub`, `isBigO_atTop_cosKernel_sub`, `isBigO_atTop_oddKernel` and `isBigO_atTop_sinKernel`, each a short proof that already calls a shared lemma `HurwitzKernelBounds.isBigO_atTop_F_*`, and the anti-unified statement (with an arbitrary function in the hole) is false. No generalised statement was checked for provability.

**So: H2 is not supported as a source of new lemmas for human-written Mathlib** (three slices, proofs ≥ 800 nodes). The library is already factored by its authors and reviewers; what a hash finds across proofs is the residue of automation. H2 holds only in the weak sense that proof *terms* are redundant (up to 12.5%), which matters for term size and checking time, not for readability.

**What this suggests for machine-generated proofs (not measured here).** The recurrence we did measure sits almost entirely in the parts of these proofs that a machine wrote: instance synthesis, simp sets, arithmetic certificates. The human-written skeleton around them barely repeats. A proof produced wholesale by a generator has no author who states a fact once and names it, so the plausible expectation is that it looks like the automation residue here (heavy, exact, same-proof repetition) rather than like the skeleton. That is a hypothesis, not a result: the measurement needed is this same pipeline run on a corpus of generated proofs (same hashes, same classes, and `Rosette.Lift` to kernel-check the factored result).

Limits of this measurement:

- The exact hash is sensitive to the order of hypotheses (local-context order) and to which variables a proof happens to use, so the same fact with permuted or extra hypotheses is not matched at level E. L1 / L2 do not fix that either. Recurrence is undercounted to that extent.
- `exact?` ran out of budget on 41 of 3860 statements (20,000k heartbeats, a tenth of Lean's default); those are left in whatever later class applies, so the substantive count is an upper bound on discoveries. The one-tactic probe uses a fixed list of ten tactics with default settings; a statement it does not close can still be a one-line consequence of the library.
- No cluster was actually factored into a shared lemma and kernel-checked; savings are bookkeeping on sizes.
- Savings add DAG sizes of removed subterms; sharing between different clusters in one theorem is counted more than once. Shares of total proof size are upper estimates.
- Candidates are Prop-typed subterms with proof DAG size ≥ 20; theorems are those with proof DAG size ≥ 800 in three slices. Nothing is claimed about other areas or short proofs.
- L1 / L2 anti-unifiers are not checked for provability (`0 < ◻` is false for `◻ = 0`).
- The class rules are heuristics read off names and pretty-printed conclusions; the top-25 tables show the statements so the labels can be audited. They were not validated on a labelled sample.

## What the exact recurrences are

Each exact recurring cluster gets the first class that applies:

- **instance** — statement is a typeclass (`DenselyOrdered ℝ`): emitted by instance synthesis
- **in-mathlib** — a theorem with exactly this statement (same hash) already exists
- **exact?** — `rfl` closes the statement, or `exact?` closes it from the library within the heartbeat budget
- **no-lemma** — no occurrence's proof mentions any theorem constant (constructors, projections, recursors only)
- **one-lemma** — every occurrence's proof mentions at most one theorem constant, and some mention one
- **automation-glue** — conclusion is an equation between propositions (`… = True`, `(a ∧ b) = (c ∧ d)`) or a heterogeneous equality (`≍`, congruence lemmas generated on the fly), or every occurrence is headed by congruence / `Eq.mpr` / tactic-framework glue
- **tactic-certificate** — every occurrence's proof mentions tactic-framework constants (`Mathlib.Tactic.Ring.*`, `Linarith.*`, `Mathlib.Meta.NormNum.*`, `Lean.Omega.*`, …): the output of one `ring` / `linarith` / `norm_num` / `omega` call
- **side-goal** — short positivity / nonvanishing / finiteness conclusion (`0 < x`, `x ≠ 0`, `x ≠ ⊤`)
- **in-mathlib-L1** — a library theorem has the same statement up to instance terms, literals, universes
- **one-tactic** — none of the above, but one call of `simp`, `norm_num`, `ring`, `fun_prop`, `positivity`, `omega`, `linarith`, `simp_all`, `measurability` or `aesop` closes the statement (probe pass; only run on statements that reach this point)
- **substantive** — none of the above
