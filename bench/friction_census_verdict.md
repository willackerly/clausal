# Friction census: claims A–D and the "unfolding" claim, measured

Source of this section: `bench/friction_census_verdict.md`, hand-written; every number in braces there is filled from `census.json` (or, for the robustness figures, `robust.json`) when `bench/friction_census.py` runs, so the figures cannot drift from the tables below and from `ROBUSTNESS.md`. Cells are `value [95% bootstrap interval over theorems]`.

**Revised 2026-11-10 after an adversarial review.** The first version of this verdict gave single headline figures for claim D and for the regularity excess, called the unfolding claim "not supported", and said the machine corpus has no function-with-facts packets. Those statements did not survive; the changes are marked "revised" below and the evidence is in `ROBUSTNESS.md`.

Three corpora in Lean 4 with Mathlib: **ns**, OpenAI's machine-generated Navier–Stokes/Euler formalization ({{n @ ns}} theorems); **Carleson**, human-written harmonic analysis ({{n @ carleson}}); **PFR**, human-written combinatorics and entropy ({{n @ pfr}}). The primary comparison is ns against Carleson.

## Result

Of the four places the earlier chat said proofs bloat: B (casts) and C (domain surgery) are close to absent from the machine corpus under every cut tried; they describe Carleson, the human corpus. A (constants) is present and larger than first reported once the project's own predicates are opened: the machine corpus hides `∃ C` witnesses inside home-made predicates. D (side conditions) is present, but its size depends on two counting choices and ranges from about {{r:rows/i.D/ns @ p}}% to {{r:rows/o.D_all/ns @ p}}% of proof mass; the part the chat named (measurability, integrability) is small overall because few ns theorems are about integrals, and where they are it costs what it costs in Carleson. Whether machine proofs unfold what humans keep bundled cannot be told from this data.

## What "proof mass" and "hypothesis" mean here

- **Hypothesis**: a Prop-valued binder of the theorem statement (explicit hypotheses and Prop-valued instance arguments). Each gets one class from its outermost shape or head constant; the classes are listed in `bench/friction_families.json`.
- **Proof mass**: distinct nodes of the kernel proof term after opening binders. A node is charged to a side-condition class if it lies inside a subterm whose goal is of that class ("outer" label), and separately to the class of the nearest enclosing goal ("inner" label). Full definition in `README.md`.
- **Side-condition classes**: finiteness, positivity, measurability, integrability, regularity, support. Claim B maps to finiteness and to anything mentioning a cast function; C to support and to anything mentioning indicator / restrict / support / cutoff; D to measurability, integrability and regularity; A to positivity and to the constant patterns of section 3.
- Proof terms are what the kernel checks, not what the author typed. One `simp` call can produce thousands of nodes. Shares of proof mass are shares of elaborated work, not of source lines.

## Claim A: implied-constant tracking — supported as a pattern, moderate in size (revised)

What the chat said: explicit constants and `∃ C, 0 < C ∧ …` witnesses are threaded through estimates.

- **The `∃ C` pattern exists in the machine corpus and is almost absent from the human ones.** {{A.conclEC @ ns}}% of ns theorems ({{prop.producers @ ns}}) conclude with a real-valued ∃ that carries a `0 < C` or `0 ≤ C` conjunct; Carleson has {{prop.producers @ carleson}} such theorem and PFR none. Requiring in addition that `C` appears as an upper bound gives {{A.ecBoth @ ns}}%. It is concentrated in the Navier–Stokes half ({{A.conclEC @ ns/NavierStokes}}%) and rare in Euler ({{A.conclEC @ ns/Euler}}%).
- **The constants are threaded.** {{prop.producers_consumed @ ns}} of the {{prop.producers @ ns}} are used by another project theorem and {{prop.producers_consumed_by_producer @ ns}} by another `∃ C` theorem. A theorem whose proof uses an `∃ C` lemma concludes with `∃ C` itself in {{A.prop.cond @ ns}}% of cases, against {{A.prop.base @ ns}}% otherwise. The longest chain of `∃ C` lemmas feeding `∃ C` lemmas is {{prop.longest_chain @ ns}} lemmas long.
- **Direct `∃ C` theorems hold {{mass.EC @ ns}}% of proof mass.**
- **More are hidden inside the project's own predicates (revised).** The first version treated project-defined predicates as opaque. Opened at Expr level (`ROBUSTNESS.md` section 5), {{r:preds/ns/pred_with_exC}} of the {{r:preds/ns/predicates}} ns predicates contain a real `∃ C` with a sign conjunct; `DiagonalResidual.JetRate` is `∃ C, 0 ≤ C ∧ ∀ᶠ x, ‖iteratedFDeriv … f x‖ ≤ C * q x ^ r`, a home-made big-O, and `WeightedClasses.MemClass` pairs a `ContDiffOn` field with a `∀ m, ∃ C, 0 ≤ C ∧ …` field. A further {{r:preds/ns/exC_producers_hidden_shallow}} theorems conclude a predicate that hides such a constant within two levels of unfolding ({{r:preds/ns/exC_mass_pct/shallow}}% of proof mass; {{r:preds/ns/exC_producers_hidden}} and {{r:preds/ns/exC_mass_pct/deep}}% at any depth), and {{r:preds/ns/hyp_pct/exC2}}% of all ns hypotheses are such a predicate. Direct and hidden together are roughly {{r:preds/ns/exC_mass_pct/direct @ p}}% + {{r:preds/ns/exC_mass_pct/shallow @ p}}% of proof mass. Carleson has no predicate of this kind. So the machine did what the chat describes and then wrapped it: it built its own substitutes for an implied-constant notation.
- **Positive parameters are common; the proof-mass comparison says little about constants (revised).** {{A.posParam @ ns}}% of ns theorems take a real parameter together with `0 < x`, against {{A.posParam @ carleson}}% in Carleson; positivity is {{hyp.cls.positivity @ ns}}% of ns hypotheses against {{hyp.cls.positivity @ carleson}}%. Most of these parameters are not constants in the chat's sense (the commonest binder names are `a`, `h`, `c`, `τ`, `T`, `δ`, `ε`); with a name-based filter for constants (`C*`, `K*`, `M*`, `c*`) the figure is {{A.posParamCname @ ns}}% against {{A.posParamCname @ carleson}}%. Proof mass on positivity goals is {{mass.outer.positivity @ ns}}% in ns and {{mass.outer.positivity @ carleson}}% in Carleson (difference {{mass.outer.positivity @ ns-carleson @ d}}); with the instance arguments of numerals (`NeZero`, `Nat.AtLeastTwo`) taken out it is {{r:inst/positivity/ns/without}}% and {{r:inst/positivity/carleson/without}}% (difference {{r:inst/positivity/ns-carleson without @ d}}). The review's sample also found output of `norm_num` / `linarith` and inequalities written as `0 ≤ b - a` inside this class, so the equality of the two figures is not evidence about implied constants either way.
- **Numeric literals do not separate the corpora** once the literal 2 is set aside: a real literal ≥ 3 occurs in {{A.litReal3 @ ns}}% of ns statements and {{A.litReal3 @ carleson}}% of Carleson's. Any real literal ≥ 2: {{A.litReal @ ns}}% against {{A.litReal @ carleson}}%.
- **Carleson handles constants a third way that the chat did not mention**: as named definitions (`C2_1_2 a`, `defaultD a`). {{A.named1 @ carleson}}% of Carleson statements, and {{A.named1 @ carleson/main}}% outside its `ToMathlib` folder, mention a project-defined real constant; ns {{A.named1 @ ns}}%.

Not tested: the chat's causal clause, that this happens because Mathlib lacks a multi-parameter "≲". The data show which of three styles each project used (∃-witness, parameter with positivity hypothesis, named definition), not what a "≲" would save.

## Claim B: the ℝ / ℝ≥0 / ℝ≥0∞ cast tax — not supported for the machine corpus

- The machine corpus barely uses ℝ≥0∞. Finiteness is {{hyp.cls.finiteness @ ns}}% of hypotheses; a cast function occurs in {{hyp.B.cast @ ns}}% of hypotheses; {{thm.S_B @ ns}}% of statements have a cast or a finiteness fact; finiteness goals take {{mass.B @ ns}}% of proof mass; cast, ℝ≥0∞ and finiteness lemmas are {{const.B @ ns}}% of library constant uses.
- A generous upper bound, counting the whole proof of anything whose goal mentions a cast, is {{mass.B.region @ ns}}%.
- In Carleson the tax is visible: {{thm.S_B @ carleson}}% of statements, {{hyp.B @ carleson}}% of hypotheses, {{mass.B @ carleson}}% of proof mass on finiteness goals, {{const.B @ carleson}}% of library constant uses. The upper bound is {{mass.B.region @ carleson}}%. The true cost in Carleson lies between about 2% and 43% and this export cannot narrow it: the region label is inherited from the statement, so a theorem stated with one `ENNReal.ofReal` puts its whole proof in the region.
- Restricting both corpora to theorems that mention measure theory does not close the gap: upper bound {{mass.B.region @ ns|MT}}% against {{mass.B.region @ carleson|MT}}%.

So B describes a human analysis library built on ℝ≥0∞-valued integrals; it is not where the machine corpus is large.

## Claim D: side-condition leakage — partly supported, size not robust (revised)

- **D is present in the machine corpus; its size depends on the rule (revised).** On the first version's rule it is {{mass.D @ ns}}% of proof mass and {{hyp.D @ ns}}% of hypotheses ({{thm.hasD @ ns}}% of theorems have at least one such hypothesis), against {{mass.D @ carleson}}% and {{hyp.D @ carleson}}% in Carleson. The table under "Result" gives the four rule combinations: {{r:rows/i.D/ns @ p}}% to {{r:rows/o.D_all/ns @ p}}% in ns, {{r:rows/i.D/carleson @ p}}% to {{r:rows/o.D_all/carleson @ p}}% in Carleson. On three of the four the ns excess is between {{r:rows/o.D/ns-carleson @ p}} and {{r:rows/i.D_all/ns-carleson @ p}} points.
- **Its content in ns is smoothness and derivative computation.** Regularity is {{mass.outer.regularity @ ns}}% of mass on the outer label, of which {{r:rows/o.dv/ns}} points are derivative-value facts (`HasDerivAt`), and {{hyp.cls.regularity @ ns}}% of hypotheses. Measurability and integrability together are {{mass.D.mi @ ns}}% of mass and {{hyp.D.mi @ ns}}% of hypotheses. In Carleson the proportions are reversed (measurability + integrability {{mass.D.mi @ carleson}}% of mass, {{hyp.D.mi @ carleson}}% of hypotheses; regularity {{mass.outer.regularity @ carleson}}%), and in PFR measurability alone is {{hyp.cls.measurability @ pfr}}% of hypotheses.
- **Most of the ns regularity mass is in lemmas whose own conclusion is a regularity fact**, not in side goals inside other proofs. Side conditions discharged inline take {{mass.inline @ ns}}% of ns mass and {{mass.inline @ carleson}}% of Carleson's (difference {{mass.inline @ ns-carleson @ d}}); inline regularity is {{mass.inline.regularity @ ns}}%, inline measurability {{mass.inline.measurability @ ns}}%, inline integrability {{mass.inline.integrability @ ns}}%. Inside those stand-alone lemmas only {{r:rows/rr.i_reg/ns @ p}}% of nodes sit directly under a regularity goal; {{r:rows/rr.i_eq/ns @ p}}% are under equation goals and {{r:rows/rr.k2/ns @ p}}% are instance arguments, which is why the inner label gives about half the outer figure.
- **The small measurability / integrability share is a composition effect (revised).** The first version said a packet of measurability, integrability and finiteness "would address at most" {{mass.D.mi @ ns}}% + {{mass.B @ ns}}% of ns proof mass. That is the corpus-wide share, and it is small because only {{r:matched/matched/n/ns}} ns theorems ({{r:matched/matched/ns_share_of_nodes}}% of proof nodes) state something about an integral or an Lp norm without a derivative. Inside that stratum ns spends {{r:matched/matched/mi/ns}}% of proof mass on measurability and integrability against {{r:matched/matched/mi/carleson}}% in Carleson (difference {{r:matched/matched/mi/ns-carleson @ d}}); with finiteness added, {{r:matched/matched/mif/ns}}% against {{r:matched/matched/mif/carleson}}% ({{r:matched/matched/mif/ns-carleson @ d}}); with instance synthesis taken out, {{r:matched/matched/mif_noinst/ns}}% against {{r:matched/matched/mif_noinst/carleson}}%. So where the machine corpus does integration it pays about what the human corpus pays, roughly a tenth of proof mass. This is the claim-D cost the chat described, and it is not machine-specific.
- **The machine corpus built its own function-with-facts predicates (revised).** {{U.packet @ ns}}% of ns statements mention a project-defined structure that carries data fields together with proof fields, and {{U.bundle @ ns}}% mention that or a project Prop-valued structure; Carleson {{U.packet @ carleson}}% and {{U.bundle @ carleson}}%. The first version called these "specific to the construction, not reusable function-with-facts packets". That was wrong for a large part of them: {{r:preds/ns/pred_with_D_atom}} of the {{r:preds/ns/predicates}} project predicates contain a regularity, measurability or integrability fact, and {{r:preds/ns/hyp_pct/reg}}% of all ns hypotheses are a project predicate containing a smoothness fact ({{r:preds/ns/hyp_pct/regexC}}% one that pairs smoothness with a hidden constant: `MemClass`, `MeanClass`, `UniformClass`, `PolynomialJets`). They bundle smoothness with bounds, not measurability with integrability ({{r:preds/ns/hyp_pct/mi}}% of hypotheses). Theorems that conclude such a smoothness-carrying predicate hold {{r:preds/ns/concl_mass_pct/reg}}% of proof mass, all counted as main-line proof above. The ns support library uses almost no project bundles ({{U.bundle @ ns/support}}%).

## The unfolding claim — cannot tell (revised)

What the chat said: machine-written proofs unfold abstractions that human proofs keep. The first version's verdict was "not supported". The evidence supports less than that. Four of the five proxies below describe *statements*, and the claim is about *proofs*. On statements the machine corpus is as bundled as Carleson or more so. The one proof-level trace (P5, equation-lemma rewrites) cannot see the usual ways of unfolding. So the claim is neither supported nor refuted here.

| proxy | why it measures the claim | ns | Carleson | reading |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 limits: statements using filters or asymptotics, against statements with an explicit `∀ ε > 0, … ∃ …, … < ε` | an unfolded limit is an ε-statement | {{U.lim.bundled @ ns}}% bundled, {{U.lim.epsStrict @ ns}}% explicit | {{U.lim.bundled @ carleson}}%, {{U.lim.epsStrict @ carleson}}% | no sign of unfolding |
| P2 maps: bundled morphism types against `IsLinearMap`-style predicates; share of term binders that are bare functions | an unfolded linear map is a function plus hypotheses | {{U.lin.bundled @ ns}}% bundled, {{U.lin.unbundled @ ns @ p}}% predicate; {{U.bt.fun @ ns}}% bare functions | {{U.lin.bundled @ carleson}}%, {{U.lin.unbundled @ carleson @ p}}%; {{U.bt.fun @ carleson}}% | machine more bundled |
| P3 Lp: `eLpNorm` / `MemLp` / `Lp` against an integral of a power of a norm | an unfolded Lp norm is that integral | {{U.lp.bundled @ ns}}% bundled, {{U.lp.raw @ ns}}% raw | {{U.lp.bundled @ carleson}}%, {{U.lp.raw @ carleson}}% | both bundled; Carleson uses Lp more and raw integrals more |

- P4 is the only proxy pointing the chat's way, and it is not stable under the subject controls: among theorems mentioning measure theory the certificate share is {{const.cert @ ns|MT}}% in ns against {{const.cert @ carleson|MT}}% in Carleson. The large gap is between the support libraries ({{const.cert @ ns/support}}% against {{const.cert @ carleson/ToMathlib}}%).
- P5's equation-lemma count sees `rw [foo]` and recursive unfolding. It does not see `simp [foo]` or `unfold foo` on a non-recursive definition, `dsimp`, `show` or `change`, which unfold by definitional equality and leave no trace in the term. Neither "the machine unfolds less" nor "the machine does not unfold more" is established.
- **A source-level cross-check points the chat's way.** This is a count of tactic names in the source files, which this census otherwise does not use, reproduced after the review; it is a pointer, not a measurement. Per `rw [` call, ns uses `change` 0.31 times against 0.02 in Carleson and `dsimp` 0.11 against 0.01. Per theorem, ns calls the side-condition automation `fun_prop` / `measurability` / `continuity` 0.010 times against 0.20, and `gcongr` 0.009 against 0.46. So the machine scripts restate goals by definitional unfolding far more per rewrite and discharge side conditions and monotonicity by hand far more often.
- Settling the claim needs the tactic-level record of each proof (Lean's InfoTrees): which definitions each step unfolds and which side goals each step closes by automation.

## Subject-matter control, and what cannot be concluded

The three corpora are about different mathematics, so a raw difference between ns and Carleson mixes author with subject. Four checks were made (section 5 below, and `ROBUSTNESS.md` section 3 for the last):

- **Loose content stratum.** Restricted to theorems whose statement mentions any `Mathlib.MeasureTheory` constant ({{n @ ns|MT}} in ns, {{n @ carleson|MT}} in Carleson), the regularity excess remains ({{mass.outer.regularity @ ns|MT}}% against {{mass.outer.regularity @ carleson|MT}}%), the cast gap remains, and the instance-argument excess grows ({{mass.kind.inst @ ns|MT}}% against {{mass.kind.inst @ carleson|MT}}%).
- **Tighter matched stratum (revised).** Statement mentions `eLpNorm` / `MemLp` / `Lp` or an integral, and no derivative or `ContDiff` constant ({{r:matched/matched/n/ns}} ns theorems, {{r:matched/matched/n/carleson}} in Carleson). The regularity excess vanishes ({{r:matched/matched/reg_all/ns}}% against {{r:matched/matched/reg_all/carleson}}%, difference {{r:matched/matched/reg_all/ns-carleson @ d}}), so the first version's statement that it "survives all three subject controls" was wrong: the loose stratum was not a control for it. Measurability + integrability is at parity or slightly higher in ns ({{r:matched/matched/mi/ns-carleson @ d}}). The cast gap survives (finiteness {{r:matched/matched/fin/ns}}% against {{r:matched/matched/fin/carleson}}%; cast region {{r:matched/matched/cast/ns}}% against {{r:matched/matched/cast/carleson}}%), and so does the instance excess ({{r:matched/matched/inst/ns-carleson @ d}}).
- **Support library against support library** (ns modules that import no project module, {{n @ ns/support}} theorems, against `Carleson.ToMathlib`, {{n @ carleson/ToMathlib}}): regularity {{mass.outer.regularity @ ns/support}}% against {{mass.outer.regularity @ carleson/ToMathlib}}%; finiteness {{mass.outer.finiteness @ ns/support}}% against {{mass.outer.finiteness @ carleson/ToMathlib}}%.
- **Second human corpus.** PFR differs from Carleson by as much as ns does on many rows (measurability {{hyp.cls.measurability @ pfr}}% of hypotheses against {{hyp.cls.measurability @ carleson}}%; positivity {{hyp.cls.positivity @ pfr}}% against {{hyp.cls.positivity @ carleson}}%). Human-to-human variation across subjects is as large as the machine-to-human differences reported here.

What follows:

- The profile of the machine corpus is established: where its hypotheses and proof mass are, by class, with intervals.
- No difference between ns and Carleson can be attributed to machine authorship. There is one machine project and one human analysis project; they differ in subject (PDE blow-up construction with explicit smooth profiles against harmonic analysis on doubling metric measure spaces), in Lean and Mathlib version, and in conventions (Carleson states its estimates with ℝ≥0∞-valued integrals; ns stays in ℝ). The regularity excess is a subject effect by the matched comparison above. The instance-argument excess survives the subject and size controls but not a version control, which was not possible.
- The intervals cover sampling of theorems within a corpus only. They say nothing about how another machine-written or human-written project would look. Section 6 repeats the key rows resampling whole modules; the intervals widen, most for Carleson, and no verdict changes.
- The claims are about *bloat*, which implies a counterfactual: how long the proof would be with better abstractions. A census measures where mass is, not how much of it is avoidable.

## Limits of the measurements

- **Only theorems are covered by the tables below (revised).** Proof obligations of definitions, instances and structures (`foo._proof_N`) are outside `kind == theorem`. Exported separately (`ROBUSTNESS.md` section 6) they are {{r:aux/ns/aux_share_of_all_proof_nodes}}% of all ns proof nodes ({{r:aux/carleson/aux_share_of_all_proof_nodes}}% in Carleson, {{r:aux/pfr/aux_share_of_all_proof_nodes}}% in PFR). Their class mix is close to that of the theorems (claim D {{r:aux/ns/D_all_aux}}% against {{r:aux/ns/D_all_theorems}}%; combined {{r:aux/ns/D_all_combined}}%), except that they are {{r:aux/ns/kind_inst}}% instance arguments. No verdict changes when they are included.
- **Side-condition classes include instance synthesis.** `NeZero` and `Nat.AtLeastTwo` arguments of numerals count as positivity, `BorelSpace` instances as measurability, `IsFiniteMeasure` instances as finiteness. With Prop-valued instance arguments taken out (`ROBUSTNESS.md` section 2), ns measurability falls from {{r:inst/measurability/ns/with @ p}}% to {{r:inst/measurability/ns/without @ p}}% of proof mass and PFR finiteness from {{r:inst/finiteness/pfr/with @ p}}% to {{r:inst/finiteness/pfr/without @ p}}%; other rows move by under half a point.

- Classes are syntactic. `0 ≤ b - a` counts as positivity; a regularity fact hidden inside a project-defined predicate is `other` ({{hyp.cls.other @ ns}}% of ns hypotheses, {{hyp.projHead @ ns}}% have a project-defined head), so D is undercounted in ns by an unknown amount.
- The `∃ C` detector accepts any real-valued ∃ with a sign conjunct; a hash-ordered sample (section 8) contains a time budget `∃ T, 0 < T ∧ T ≤ Tmax ∧ …` that is not an implied constant. The stricter "used as an upper bound" row is the conservative figure.
- "Positive real parameter" includes viscosities, times, radii and ε. The name filter is a heuristic on binder names.
- "Named real constant" means a project definition with result type ℝ, ℝ≥0 or ℝ≥0∞ whose arguments are all ℕ or ℤ; it misses constants that take a real argument and the wider row includes ordinary real functions.
- The support-library split for ns is by import graph (modules importing no project module); the repository has no folder that marks a support library, and Navier–Stokes and Euler do not import each other.
- Proof-side attribution was verified by the export step only through its invariants and six small proofs. The new detectors added for this census (ε-shape, integral of a power, `∃ C` used as a bound, literals) were checked by reading hash-ordered samples of their hits against the stored statements, not exhaustively; one false-positive pattern in the ε-shape detector was found that way and fixed.
