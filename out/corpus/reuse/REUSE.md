# Recurrence in a machine-generated corpus (OpenAI Navier–Stokes / Euler)

Generated by `bench/corpus_reuse.py report`. Numbers come from Lean dumps (`out/corpus/ns/decls.jsonl`, `out/corpus/reuse/stmt_keys.jsonl`, `out/corpus/reuse/inproof_*/`, `out/corpus/reuse/factor/*.jsonl`); the judgements on clusters are hand-entered in `out/corpus/reuse/judgements.json` and marked as such. Corpus: `openai/NavierStokesAndEuler` at `f9e8bc5`, Lean 4.34.0-rc2, 38601 theorems, 640857 source lines (448391 of them inside theorem declarations), total theorem proof size 48624258 DAG nodes.

## Verdict

**Is there materially more redundancy in the machine corpus than in Mathlib? Of the kind this project was looking for, no. Of two other kinds, yes.**

1. *Inside proofs, the comparable figure is of the same size, and small.* Non-trivial recurring statements are worth **0.15%** of proof size in the dense sample (501 theorems of neighbouring modules) and **0.03%** in the uniform sample (500 theorems), against **0.20%** for human-written Mathlib. Counting inline re-proofs of the project's own named lemmas as redundancy does not change either figure (0.15%, 0.03%). (The traversal used here differs from the Mathlib run in a way that can only lower the machine figures, see section 2; the comparison is of magnitudes, not of third digits.) 17 statements in the dense sample would save anything; 14 of them have the identical proof term at every occurrence and 5 cross a module boundary. They are side conditions (`t ∈ Ioo 0 T → t ≤ T`, an `eventually` membership, arithmetic on indices), not lemmas. The hypothesis recorded in `out/reuse/REUSE.md`, that a generator which never names a fact would repeat substantive sub-proofs, is **not supported**.
2. *Raw term-level recurrence is two to three times higher, and it is automation residue.* Replacing every repeated closed statement by a call would remove at most 29.4% (dense) and 33.4% (uniform) of the proof terms, against 12.5% in Mathlib; 98.1% of the recurring statements fall in a trivial class (`ring` normal forms, typeclass instances, statements `exact?` closes). This matters for term size and checking time, not for readability, and the DAG-aware traversal used here can only understate it.
3. *At declaration level, exact duplication is at the level of human projects.* 363 of 38601 theorems (0.9%) restate another theorem exactly; the same count on the human blueprint corpora gives 0.7% (PFR) and 0.5% (Carleson) with their exporter's hash, which is universe-sensitive and gives 357 here. 357 of the 363 were re-proved through the kernel by a bare reference to another copy.
4. *What is specific to the machine corpus is copy-and-modify instead of generalise.* (a) Thirteen modules are namespace-renamed copies of other modules (7308 lines; the largest is a 5,573-line copy), all of them dead. (b) 733 clusters of theorems outside those modules are the same derivation from parallel ingredients (same statement and proof term up to renaming project constants): the same estimate for `q ≥ 6` and `q ≥ 5`, for Sobolev order 3 and 6, for a narrow and a wide parameter range, for the angular and the axial component; by a hand-read sample most of them are sibling lemmas of one to four lines. (c) A private helper is restated in 82 modules. Statement shape alone is misleading here: 17.5% of theorems share their statement up to renaming, but 18 of the 20 largest such clusters are families like `0 < c` or `ContDiff ℝ ∞ f` for different definitions, whose common generalisation is false.
5. *The factoring goes through.* Six experiments, 468 distinct theorems re-derived and accepted by the kernel. Two needed a new general lemma, written here and compiled at the first attempt (a 39-line Sobolev estimate for arbitrary order; a one-line power inequality). Two needed none, because the corpus already contains the general statement and proves the special case again. Failures are reported: 3 of 21 pairs in one family have different constants in the conclusion, and 144 of 312 cloned theorems mention a cloned structure and cannot be proved by the original.
6. *How much could a deduplication / generalisation pass remove?* With what these keys can find, at most **1447 theorems = 3.7% of theorems, 2.4% of theorem source lines, 2.0% of proof size**, and that is optimistic (a call replaces each removed member; the median member of the non-duplicate clusters is four lines long). The part verified by the kernel is 468 theorems. For scale: 12516 theorems (32.4%) are not used by any of the four headline theorems at all, so removing dead declarations is an order of magnitude larger than anything deduplication offers. Redundancy is not what makes this corpus hard to read.

## 1. Declaration level: all theorem statements of the corpus

### 1.1 Keys

Every key is a 64-bit structural hash of the theorem's type computed by `corpora/ns_reuse/StmtKeys.lean` from the built environment. All keys ignore binder names, binder kinds, `mdata` and universe levels.

- **E** exact statement.
- **N** numeric literals abstracted: `@OfNat.ofNat T n inst` becomes `NUM(T)`, any other raw literal `LIT`.
- **L** N, plus every constant standing without arguments in an argument position is a numbered hole (first-occurrence numbering, so the renaming is consistent). One level of leaf abstraction: types, instances and named constants are leaves, heads of applications are kept.
- **P** N, plus every constant declared in the project is a numbered hole wherever it occurs, heads included. Equal P means: same statement after renaming project definitions and changing numerals. A head is a second-order parameter, so the common generalisation need not be true.
- **LP** both. This is the approximation of anti-unification used below.
- **+perm** the same key made invariant under reordering of binders: a binder whose variable occurs nowhere else (a hypothesis, an unused variable) is *floating*; the key hashes the conclusion, the other binder types with variables numbered by first occurrence, and the sorted multiset of floating binders. Equal keys imply equality up to binder order; the numbering is a heuristic, so some pairs can be missed.
- **SV-E / SV-N / SV-P** joint keys of statement AND proof term: identical; identical up to numerals; identical up to one consistent renaming of project constants in statement and proof, where each renamed constant must also agree in kind and in the shape of its own type. SV-P says the two theorems are the same derivation from parallel ingredients.

- **Exact duplicates are rare**: 363 removable theorems (0.9%), 0.55% of proof size. Only 211 of them also have an identical proof term; the rest are the same statement proved again by another route.
- The four largest binder-order-only clusters are a theorem and its own `_aux` restatement with the binders in another order, i.e. not independent proofs.
- **Permuted hypotheses add little**: 33 clusters (70 theorems) are equal only up to binder order.
- **Numerals add little**: 75 clusters (156 theorems) differ only in numeric literals; in 31 of them the proofs also agree up to renaming, which is the pattern "same estimate proved for several exponents".
- **Renaming project constants adds a lot, and most of it is not redundancy**: P has 2534 clusters covering 6688 theorems (17.3%). Of the 2569 LP clusters, 875 have the same proof up to renaming and 1518 have pairwise different proofs. See 1.4.

## 2. Inside proofs: sampled theorems

Method of `out/reuse/REUSE.md` (the Mathlib run): every Prop-typed proof subterm with proof DAG size ≥ 20 is closed over the local hypotheses it uses; closed statements are hashed; a statement is *recurring* if it occurs in ≥ 2 theorems; each recurring statement gets the first trivial class that applies; the headline is the net saving of the statements no filter explains, as a share of total proof size. The Lean code is `Rosette/Candidates.lean` + `Rosette/Reuse.lean` copied into `corpora/ns_reuse/ReuseNS.lean` by `mk_reuse_ns.py` (it compiled on Lean 4.34 without API changes); the classification is `bench/reuse.py`, unchanged.

- **Population**: theorems with proof DAG size ≥ 800, the Mathlib threshold: 16885 of 38601 theorems, carrying 84.7% of all theorem proof size.
- **Uniform sample**: 500 drawn uniformly (seed 20261109).
- **Dense sample**: all eligible theorems of consecutive modules (sorted by name) from a seeded start, until ≥ 250 per library: 251 from 14 NavierStokes modules (`PositiveRepresentatives` … `PrimaryPulseBounds`), 250 from 74 Euler modules. The Mathlib run took all eligible theorems of three directory slices, so the dense sample is the like-for-like one; the uniform sample shows what recurrence between unrelated theorems looks like.
- **Differences from the Mathlib run**, all forced: (a) the traversal is DAG-aware. The original walks the proof term as a tree; on these terms a third of the first 129 theorems hit the 300,000-visit budget with proof DAG sizes of 1–3 thousand. Here a subterm met again inside one theorem is not re-entered; if it is a candidate, its extra position is still recorded. Nested candidates lose their extra positions, which can only lower the saving. (b) `exact?` searches the whole environment, which here includes the project's own lemmas; the columns "re-proofs counted" undo that: a statement closed by a PROJECT lemma is not treated as trivial and falls through to the later filters. (c) The anti-unifier pass (levels L1/L2 of the Mathlib report) was not run.

## 3. Limits

- All keys are 64-bit hashes; collisions were not checked, and no key was validated on hand-made alpha-equivalent pairs.
- L and P are one fixed approximation of anti-unification. P renames heads, so equal keys do not imply a true common statement; that is why SV-P and the kernel checks are used for every claim of removability.
- SV-P requires the WHOLE proof term to be parallel. Two proofs of the same parameterised fact written differently are missed (the corpus has such pairs: the two `lam ^ n ≤ lam` lemmas of F5 are found only by the numeral key). SV-P also abstracts numerals, which produced 3 false twins in 21 in F4.
- Only theorems are clustered. Duplicated definitions, structures and instances are not counted, although the cloned modules copy 80 non-theorem declarations as well.
- The factoring experiments show that members follow from a general statement. The corpus was not rebuilt with the members deleted, so knock-on effects (simp sets, name resolution, private scope) are untested.
- In-proof: two samples of about 500 from 16,885 eligible theorems. Recurrence between theorems that are not in the same sample is invisible, so a whole-corpus figure would be higher than either sample's; the dense sample bounds this for neighbouring modules only.
- In-proof: the traversal differs from the Mathlib run (DAG-aware here), the Mathlib figures were not recomputed, and the class rules are the same name- and text-based heuristics as before.
- In-proof: `exact?` and each probe tactic ran with 20,000k heartbeats; a timeout leaves the statement in whatever later class applies, so the substantive counts are upper bounds on real discoveries.
- The judgements in 1.4 and 1.5 were made by the analysing agent, not by a mathematician.

## 4. Review notes (2026-11-10)

An independent reviewing agent recomputed the cluster table from `stmt_keys.jsonl` (all keys reproduce), the 468 kernel-accepted theorems and the E + SV-P union bound, and reran F2 to F5 on Lean 4.34.0-rc2. Its findings were all minor; none changes a conclusion. They are recorded here rather than folded into the tables.

- **The E + SV-P removal bound is about twice what is realistic.** In a seeded sample of 20 same-derivation clusters with differing statements the reviewer found 9 true copies (cloned-module pairs, `q ≥ 5`/`q ≥ 6` twins, a narrow/wide parameter pair) and 11 sibling lemmas about different definitions, where one general lemma would have to quantify over the definition and its properties; two of the 11 differ in the conclusion itself. This agrees with the 14-cluster sample in `judgements.json` (5 copies, 1 same computation for two indices, 8 siblings). Read the bound as: at most the figure in the table, realistically about half of it.
- **Two totals are in use for the in-proof "substantive" class.** In the dense sample the headline row sums 1,556 nodes of net saving (0.15% of proof size, 17 statements with positive saving); the class table sums 1,193 nodes (0.11%; the reviewer counts 14 statements with positive saving under that definition). The reviewer reports the same gap in the Mathlib column (0.20% against 0.18%). The two definitions live in `bench/reuse.py` and were not unified here because that script belongs to the Mathlib run. Either figure supports the same reading: the same magnitude as Mathlib, and small.
- **Indirect circularity.** `#follows` only checks that a derived proof does not mention the member it replaces directly. The reviewer added a transitive check over project constants to copies of F2 to F5: 31 derived theorems, none reaches its member, all types equal the member's. F1 and F6 were not rerun with that check; section 1 already notes that in 8 duplicate pairs the corpus itself proves one copy from the other.
- **Not rerun in the review:** the in-proof pipeline and the Mathlib comparison figures.
