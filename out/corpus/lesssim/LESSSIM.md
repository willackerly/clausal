# Constant threading: does one relation that hides the constant compress it?

2026-11-10. Corpus: OpenAI's Navier–Stokes/Euler formalisation (`corpora/ns`, Lean 4.34.0-rc2, Mathlib `85e3a25e`), read-only. Everything below is kernel-checked against the built corpus; no `sorry`; axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound` only. No reader ratings were collected: "readable" is reported through counts, not through a model proxy.

## Result

The longest chain of `∃ C, 0 ≤ C ∧ …` lemmas in the corpus (13 theorems) was restated with Mathlib's `f =O[𝓟 S] g`, and each restated statement was proved and shown equivalent to the original. Figures marked *(review)* come from the independent review of 2026-11-10 (`out/corpus/lesssim/review/`).

- **11 of 13 conclusions restated.** Two needed the constant unpacked: one keeps its original statement, one unpacks inside its proof. None failed. This counts conclusions, not proofs: **8 of 13 native proofs never handle an explicit constant** (steps 1, 2, 3, 4, 9, 10, 11, 13; 13 is unchanged), 3 open by unpacking an explicit constant and re-wrap it with `isBigO_of_bound_on` (steps 5, 6, 12), and 2 are the escape hatches (7, 8).
- **Proofs: 2.4% to 4.2% smaller in elaborated nodes.** 25,696 → 24,623 (−4.2%) as measured here; −2.4% (25,710 → 25,098) once the matcher definitions generated by the pattern-lambda statements are charged to the proofs that use them *(review)*. 5.7% fewer source tokens, 20% fewer lines, about the same number of characters.
- **The saving is in the steps that only pass a constant along.** Six such steps shrink 26.7%. Five steps that do analysis are unchanged (−0.8% to +1.8%). The two that meet an interface with explicit constants grow 17.5% to 19.4%. (The second figure of each pair charges the matchers.)
- **Statements: 10.6% fewer source lines (104 → 93), source tokens flat (1,427 → 1,428), lexical tokens +5.2%, characters +3.1%, and 4.8% more elaborated nodes.** The `∃ C, 0 ≤ C ∧` prefix disappears from 12 conclusions; the number of binding sites a reader meets does not fall (about 40 → 42 in the source, because the relation replaces quantifiers with `fun` and set-builder binders) *(review; an earlier version of this line said 16% fewer lines and 62 binders → 13)*. The single-parameter statements shrink 1.3%; the three that are uniform in several parameters grow 11% to 38% because the parameters must be packed into a tuple. Hypothesis counts do not change (58 → 58).
- **The library costs more than this family saves**: 15 lemmas, 8,603 proof nodes and 83 source lines, against 1,073 nodes and 46 lines saved. 5,728 of those nodes are three adapters to corpus lemmas that take explicit constants, and the corpus algebra the family stops using is not credited, so the 8:1 ratio describes piecemeal adoption and is pessimistic for migrating a closed cluster.
- **Beyond the family the saving did not repeat.** By a rule run in Lean, 478 of the 946 `∃ C` theorems (50.5%; 2.4% of all proof size) have a shape the relation covers. Of a seeded sample of 10, four were completed with the library unchanged, and their proofs came out 35% larger in nodes (2% fewer tokens); only one of the four (S4) is a proof written against the relation. There is no measured saving outside the family.
- **The corpus had already built this relation three times** (`JetRate` with `weaken`/`add`/`congr_on`; `weaken_bound`/`add_bounds`; `finite_polynomial_majorant`) without sharing it. `JetRate` is proved here to be equivalent to `IsBigO` when `q ^ r` is eventually nonnegative, which holds throughout the corpus.

The trade, stated plainly, is at the end.

## 1. The family, chosen by rule before any design

**Rule.** Producers are the census set: the 946 theorems whose conclusion has a real `∃` with a sign conjunct. Depth is the longest producer-to-producer chain through proof dependencies (this reproduces the census histogram: maximum 13, three heads). The head is the deepest producer, ties broken by smallest full name. The family is one longest path down from it, taking at each step the direct producer dependency one level lower, ties by smallest name. Recorded in `DECISIONS.md` before the relation was designed; scripts `corpora/lesssim/chain.py`, `family.py`; list in `family.json`.

## 2. What Mathlib already offers

**It fits, so it is used.** `Asymptotics.isBigO_principal` says

```lean
f =O[𝓟 s] g ↔ ∃ c, ∀ x ∈ s, ‖f x‖ ≤ c * ‖g x‖
```

- **Uniform in some parameters, dependent on others.** The variable of `f` and `g` ranges over the parameters the constant must be uniform in (a tuple when there are several) and `s` is the set of admissible tuples. Whatever the constant may depend on is bound outside the relation. Nothing else is needed: this is exactly the corpus's shape.
- **Mathlib uses the same idiom**: `IsBigO.sum_congr'` and `IsBigOWith.sum_congr'` state uniformity in an index with the filter `⊤ ×ˢ l`.
- **Algebra already there**: `isBigO_refl`, `IsBigO.trans`, `.add`, `.mul`, `.smul`, `.pow`, `.sum` / `.fun_sum`, `.mono` (smaller set), `.comp_tendsto` (substitution), `.congr'`, `.norm_left`, `ContinuousOn.isBigO_principal` (continuous on a compact set).
- **Nothing newer helps.** The corpus's Mathlib has `IsBigOWith`, `IsTheta`, `IsBigOTVS`, `IsEquivalent`, `SuperpolynomialDecay`. `IsBigOWith` keeps the constant visible; `IsBigOTVS` generalises the target space, not the parameters. There is no relation for "up to a power of a slow scale".
- **The corpus knows `IsBigO`**: 20 lines in 6 files, all for one-variable limits (`=o[𝓝 x]`, `=O[atTop]`), none for a uniform estimate.

**Where it does not fit exactly** (each met in the experiment):

| mismatch | consequence |
|---|---|
| Norms on both sides | To get the corpus's form back the right side must be known nonnegative on the set. A one-sided real bound `F x ≤ K` is equivalent only when `F ≥ 0` |
| `0 < C` in the original | Cosmetic: export with `C + 1` |
| Several uniform parameters are curried in the corpus | They must be packed in a tuple; statements carry `q.1`, `q.2.1` or a pattern-matching lambda |
| Jets of order `j ≤ m` live in a type that depends on `j` | `j` cannot be one of the uniform parameters; it stays as `∀ j ≤ m` outside, and a lemma recovers one constant for the finite family |
| A second existential, the exponent `p` of a slow scale | Not hidden: the native statements still say `∃ p` |
| The constant is an argument of a structure (`JetData … A B … p q m`) | Cannot be hidden without restating the structure and its consumer |
| Lower bounds, constants in exponents or sums, constants chosen together with a function or set | Not this relation |

## 3. The relation and its library

`corpora/lesssim/lean/Lib.lean`, 15 lemmas, 83 source lines.

- **Moving in and out** (generic): `isBigO_of_bound_on` (an explicit constant gives the relation, no sign condition), `exists_bound_on` (the escape hatch: back to `∃ C, 0 ≤ C ∧ …`), `isBigO_on_iff`.
- **Set-flavoured names for Mathlib laws**: `mono_set`, `congr_on`, `congr_left_on`, `congr_right_on`, `comp_mapsTo`, `isBigO_of_le_on`.
- **One constant for a finite family**: `exists_bound_on_finset`, `exists_bound_on_le` (the second allows the value space to depend on the index, for jets).
- **Exponent law**: `isBigO_rpow_of_le` (for a quantity in `(0, 1]`, a larger exponent is dominated by a smaller one).
- **The project's operations**: `jet_add` (jets of a sum), `jet_comp` (the chain-rule estimate as a law, uniform in a parameter that the two maps, their patches and the point may all depend on), `uniformClass_isBigO` (the project predicate `UniformClass` read as a big-O).

A native statement looks like this (step 11):

```lean
theorem WaveData.vector_bound_with_gain (W : PhysicalStageBounds.WaveData h D I K (Fin 3))
    (hh : 0 < h) (hh1 : h < 1 / 2) (hg : g ≤ h * W.alpha + W.shift + delta) (m : ℕ) :
    (fun w => iteratedFDeriv ℝ m W.vector w) =O[𝓟 {w | w ∈ preterminal ∧ physicalQ h w ≤ 1}]
      fun w => physicalQ h w ^ (g - (PhysicalGraphBounds.waveLoss h m + delta)) :=
  (Native.WaveData.vector_bound W hh hh1 m).trans
    (isBigO_rpow_of_le (fun _ hw => ⟨physicalQ_pos hh hh1 hw.1, hw.2⟩)
      (by unfold PhysicalClassBounds.physicalLoss; linarith))
```

**Nothing is weakened.** `Equiv.lean` has, for each of the 13, `Back.X` whose type is structurally identical to the original (checked in Lean) and which is proved from `Native.X` alone; and for the 12 restated ones `Fwd.X`, the native statement proved from the original. So each pair is interderivable. For shapes 1–3 the converse uses that the bounded quantity is nonnegative.

## 5. Three before/after examples in full

### Example 1 (best case, a finite sum): step 9

Original (`NavierStokes.LocalPhysicalCopyBounds`, lines 820–850):

```lean
theorem physical_vector_sum_jet_bound_of_weighted {s : StripData D} {α σ P : ℝ}
    {w : ι → ℕ → D → ℝ} {source : ι → ℕ → D → ℂ}
    (hsource : LocalSourceBounds s h α w source) {f : Fin 3 → CopyFamily H K}
    (hc : ∀ i, SupportCells (f i))
    (hchart : ∀ i, CommonChart (f i) (hc i) a b h r0 σ source)
    (hmap : ∀ i k I, MapsTo ((hchart i).map k I) ((hchart i).domain k I) s.domain)
    (hb : ∀ i, PhysicalCopyBounds.CarrierBounds (f i) (hc i) a b h r0)
    (hr : ∀ i, SupportData (f i) a b h r0 Z Δ)
    (hs : ∀ i, SmoothData (f i) a h r0)
    (hh : 0 < h) (hh1 : h < 1 / 2) (ha : 0 < a) (hZ : 0 ≤ Z) (hr0 : 0 ≤ r0)
    (hP : 1 ≤ P) (hp : ∀ i k L, |((f i).carrier k L).angular| ≤ P ∧
      |((f i).carrier k L).axial| ≤ P ∧ |((f i).carrier k L).radial| ≤ P) (m : ℕ) :
    ∃ C : ℝ, 0 ≤ C ∧ ∀ z : SpaceTime,
      z ∈ preterminal → |z.1| ≤ 1 →
      ‖iteratedFDeriv ℝ m (PhysicalCopyBounds.vectorSum f a h r0) z‖ ≤
        C * physicalQ h z ^ (h * α - PhysicalClassBounds.physicalLoss h σ m) := by
  classical
  have hbnd := fun i => physical_sum_jet_bound_of_weighted hsource (hchart i) (hmap i)
    (hb i) (hr i) (hs i) hh hh1 ha hZ hr0 hP (hp i) m
  choose C hC hbound using hbnd
  refine ⟨3 * ∑ i : Fin 3, C i,
    mul_nonneg (by norm_num) (Finset.sum_nonneg (fun i _ => hC i)), ?_⟩
  intro z hz ht
  have hq := physicalQ_pos hh hh1 hz
  have hi (i : Fin 3) : C i ≤ ∑ j : Fin 3, C j :=
    Finset.single_le_sum (fun j _ => hC j) (Finset.mem_univ i)
  have he := vectorSum_jet_bound hr hs hc ha hh hh1 hz m
    (B := (∑ i : Fin 3, C i) * physicalQ h z ^ (h * α - PhysicalClassBounds.physicalLoss h σ m))
    (fun i => (hbound i z hz ht).trans
      (mul_le_mul_of_nonneg_right (hi i) (Real.rpow_pos_of_pos hq _).le))
  exact he.trans_eq (by ring)
```

Native (`corpora/lesssim/lean/Native.lean`):

## 7. What was learned about the abstraction

- **Which law carried the saving.** Of the 3,067 nodes saved in the eight steps that shrank: multiplication by a bounded factor together with transitivity, 1,209 (steps 4, 5), of which step 4's 653 coincide with moving the `UniformClass` unpacking into `uniformClass_isBigO`, a 1,808-node library lemma used only by step 4, so this item is partly a relocation of work, not a saving; finite sums, 785 (step 9); restriction to a smaller set, 420 (step 10); lowering an exponent, 330 (step 11); substitution and rewriting on the set, 253 (steps 2, 3); 70 at step 8. The four steps that grew lost 1,994 (chain rule 594, escape hatch 902, addition with imported inputs 463, step 1 35).
- **The norm on the right is a feature.** Sign side conditions (`0 ≤ C`, `0 ≤ ε ^ α`, `0 ≤ S ^ p`) disappear from proofs: an explicit constant enters through `|C|`, and a term that vanishes is bounded for free (S3). They reappear only when exporting.
- **Uniformity in several parameters is where it is awkward.** The corpus writes `∀ l n x, x ∈ s.domain → ∀ j ≤ m, …`; the relation needs `fun q : ι × ℕ × D => …` and `{q | q.2.2 ∈ s.domain}`. Statements get longer in nodes and substitution needs the tuple map written out (step 6). A four-parameter case (S5) reads `q.2.2.1.1`. Mathlib has no curried form.
- **Three things the relation does not hide**: the exponent of a slow scale (`∃ p`, 88 of the 478 fits and 3 of the family), constants that are arguments of a structure (`JetData`), and a bound that is itself quantified (`∀ B, (jets of F ≤ B) → …`, S10).
- **Leaves do not benefit.** A step that produces its constant from compactness or from a pointwise lemma with an explicit constant (1, S7, S9) is the same proof with a wrapper.
- **The corpus's predicates are this abstraction in disguise, in three different ways:**
  - `DiagonalResidual.JetRate l q f m r` is equivalent to `(fun x => iteratedFDeriv ℝ m f x) =O[l] fun x => q x ^ r` when `q x ^ r` is eventually nonnegative, which holds throughout the corpus: proved as `Lesssim.jetRate_iff_isBigO` (`lean/JetRate.lean`). The corpus then proves `JetRate.weaken`, `JetRate.add`, `JetRate.congr_on` and defines `FiniteJetRate`: the exponent law, addition, congruence and the finite-family constant, the same four items as this library. 93 theorems mention `JetRate`, 52 `FiniteJetRate`.
  - `UniformClass` / `MemClass` / `WaveClass` (141, 182, 145 theorems) hide `∃ C, ∃ p` in a field whose bound is a definition `majorant … C p n x` taking the constant as an argument. They hide the constant in statements but have no algebra: every use opens with `obtain ⟨C, hC, p, hb⟩ := hf.bounds m` and threads by hand. One 8-line lemma reads `UniformClass` as a big-O.
  - `ActualPhysicalStageBounds.weaken_bound` and `add_bounds` take and return a whole `∃ C, …` proposition for one fixed region and scale; `ParametricKernelBounds.finite_polynomial_majorant` is the finite-family lemma again; `PolynomialGrowth` is a big-O against a power. The census counted 0.18% of theorems with an `∃ C` hypothesis; lemmas of this kind are among them.
  - `JetData` is the opposite design: constants as explicit arguments, which is what forced the escape hatch.
- **So the complexity is less in each proof than in the repetition of the algebra.** The machine re-derived fragments of one relation per file and per shape. Sharing one relation removes the re-derivations; it does not make the analytic steps shorter.

## 8. Verdict, as a trade

**What it buys**

- Pass-along steps become one or two lemma applications: −27% proof nodes on six of thirteen steps, up to −43%.
- Twelve conclusions lose the `∃ C, 0 ≤ C ∧` prefix; statements have 10.6% fewer source lines but no fewer tokens or binding sites.
- Sign side conditions leave the proofs.
- One relation in place of at least three home-made ones, with Mathlib's algebra for free.

**What it costs**

- A library: 12 generic lemmas (2,875 nodes, reusable) and project-specific laws (5,728 nodes for three; more are needed outside the family, as S5, S8, S10 show).
- Statements uniform in several parameters get larger and need tuple projections.
- Every boundary with code that takes or returns explicit constants costs an import or an export; in a mixed corpus these eat the saving (steps 7, 8, 12; S7, S9).
- Overall on the family: −2.4% to −4.2% proof nodes before the library, a net increase after it (piecemeal adoption; a closed-cluster migration that replaces the corpus's own algebra was not measured).

**Where it does not apply**

- About half of the `∃ C` theorems: several constants with other uses, constants chosen with a function or set, lower bounds, constants in sums or exponents, constants as structure arguments.
- Steps that create a constant.
- Exponents of slow scales, which stay existential.

**Size of the prize.** The `∃ C` theorems are about 5–6% of proof size; the part that fits is 2.4%; the family's rate would make the saving a few percent of that part, around 0.1% of the corpus, but there is no measured saving outside the family (the sample's completed proofs grew), so 0.1% is an optimistic ceiling, not an estimate. The value, if any, is in statements a reader can scan and in not re-proving the algebra, and neither was measured with readers.

## Limits

- One chain of 13, chosen by rule; of the other two depth-13 heads, `CycleInputs.pressure_bound`'s 31-theorem producer cone contains 8 of the 13, and `ModulatedProfileJetRates.exists_with_moment_repair_all_jets` has a 24-theorem producer cone with no overlap with the family. One sample of 10, of which 4 were completed.
- The native proofs were written by one agent in one session; a different author could do better or worse on steps 6 and 12. Proof nodes are sensitive to tactic choice (`ring` against `simp only [mul_assoc]` changed one lemma by 700 nodes). Those two replacements were made after measuring, so the figures they touch (library total, S3) are exploratory under PLAN rule 1.
- The by-rule family is the best case by construction: every step consumes the previous `∃ C`. The sample (5 of 10 at depth 1 or 2) is more representative.
- The family was re-proved inside a corpus that stays in `∃ C` form. A fully native corpus would have no imports at steps 8 and 12; that is not measured.
- Source lines favour the native proofs (lines up to 110 characters against the corpus's shorter lines); tokens and characters are the fairer counts.
