Two small pieces of money-handling code. Amounts and balances are whole numbers from 0 up to a fixed maximum, 18,446,744,073,709,551,615 (2^64 - 1, the largest unsigned 64-bit number, called 'the maximum'; a 'u64' is such a number). Nothing can be negative or above the maximum.

The wallet. A wallet holds one balance. Deposit adds an amount; if the new balance would be above the maximum, the deposit is rejected. Withdraw takes an amount off; if the amount is more than the balance, it is rejected. A wallet transfer moves an amount from a sender wallet to a receiver wallet, which is always a different wallet; it is rejected if the sender has less than the amount, or if the receiver's balance would go above the maximum. Each call either succeeds (the answer Ok) or returns one error (an Err answer). There is no list of all wallets: a rule is about the wallets it mentions.

The ledger. A ledger is a list of balances, one per account. Accounts are numbered from 0, so a ledger with three accounts has accounts 0, 1 and 2; balances are written account 0 first, as in [100, 20, 0]. A transfer names a sender account, a receiver account and an amount; 0->1:30 moves 30 from account 0 to account 1. A transfer is valid on a ledger when both accounts exist, they are different accounts, the sender's balance is at least the amount, and the receiver's balance plus the amount is at most the maximum. When a transfer is applied, the ledger either accepts it and moves the money, or rejects it. A batch is a list of transfers applied in order: it stops at the first transfer that is rejected, keeps the ones already applied, and reports how many it applied (so a batch is never 'rejected'; it only reports a count). Applying a batch as a whole is all or nothing and reports accepted or rejected. Computing the total adds up all the balances and gives nothing if the sum is above the maximum; a computation that gives a number counts as accepted. Looking up an account gives its balance, or nothing if there is no such account; a lookup that gives a value counts as accepted.

Words. Internal transfer: its sender and its receiver are both accounts of this ledger. Aggregate, or the total of some balances: their sum, added as plain numbers with no upper limit and no wrap-around. Successful: accepted.

How rules are read. 'Never' and 'always' cover every call, including calls the operation rejects, unless the rule itself narrows them (for example 'a successful transfer', 'a rejected transfer', or a condition after 'for which'). A change is judged just before and just after the operation. Every operation must also return an answer: no crash, no hang.

How an echo is laid out. The echo is printed by the tool, in fixed words, in slots:

- Applies to: which operation the rule is about and which calls are covered. 'every such call, accepted or rejected [default D2]' is what the tool fills in when the rule does not narrow the calls; 'ONLY successful calls [asked for]' or 'ONLY rejected calls [asked for]' appears when the tool understood the rule to narrow them.
- Assuming: the conditions under which the rule applies; 'nothing: every state and every input' when there are none.
- Then: what must hold. 'the call gives an answer [default D1]' is filled in by the tool whenever every call is covered.
- Accepted: which answers of this operation count as an acceptance.
- Defaults: the general reading rules that were applied (D1, D2, ...). Not default: a general rule the tool understood the sentence to switch off.
- A rule with several clauses ('..., and ...') is echoed clause by clause ('clause 1: ...').

What does not count as a difference. Lines marked [default ...] and the Defaults lines restate the reading rules above. Notes in round brackets that only say what kind of value something is, or what a word gives when an account is missing, are fixed conventions of the tool: '(a u64: 0 to 2^64 - 1)', '(sender->receiver:amount, ...)', '(0 if there is no such account)', '(absent if there is no such account)'. None of these makes an echo unfaithful. Everything else does count: which operation, which calls are covered, which conditions are assumed, which quantity or which account is spoken of, and whether a comparison is strict.
